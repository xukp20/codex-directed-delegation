# Research and synthesis

Use for broad source inspection, extraction, comparison, and summaries under explicit questions. The lead owns synthesis that requires consequential interpretation beyond the assigned criteria.

## Assignment additions

Specify the source corpus or search boundary, questions, inclusion/exclusion rules, source-quality requirements, and requested comparison dimensions. Include dates or versions when they change meaning. State whether exhaustive coverage or a bounded search is required.

```text
Inspect the supplied source collection for answers to these three questions: [...].
Separate current documented behavior, proposals, and unsupported claims.
For each answer, record the source and exact section/page, plus contradictory evidence.
Return a compact comparison and the inventory of sources checked or unavailable.
Do not choose the final product approach; identify the tradeoffs the lead must decide.
```

## Return and acceptance

Return findings linked to source locations, coverage, disagreements, and missing evidence. Keep large inventories in an artifact only when useful; summarize the consequential findings in the response. Avoid dumping full documents back to the lead.

Check that citations support the actual claim and versions are comparable. A failed search is not proof of absence. Preserve qualifications when compressing material; do not turn repeated secondary claims into independent confirmation.
